# Wyatt National — Course Design

**Par 72 · 7,214 yards · Staunton, Virginia.** One course, designed like a championship venue. Four par 3s, four par 5s, ten par 4s — two of each short/long hole on each nine. Every hole has a distinct identity and a clear strategic question. Doglegs both ways, elevation throughout, a little water in play, and bunkering that punishes the wrong miss, not the good shot.

## Design principles

- **Strategy over length.** The best holes ask a question off the tee: challenge the bunker for the angle, or play safe and accept the harder approach.
- **The miss matters.** Greens are guarded on the side the approach comes from; the safe miss is always short-side or front, never long.
- **Variety by design.** No two par 4s play alike; par 3s range from a flick wedge to a long iron over water; par 5s offer genuine risk-reward second shots.
- **Wind and elevation are characters.** Uphill into the wind demands a knockdown; downwind invites the high ball. The course plays differently every round.

## Par breakdown

- Par 3s: 3, 8, 12, 16 — two per nine, ranging 164–226 yards.
- Par 5s: 2, 7, 11, 15 — two per nine, ranging 545–591 yards.
- Par 4s: 1, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 13, 14, 17, 18 — ranging 376–455 yards.

## What makes it great

- **Every hole asks a question.** There is no filler hole.
- **The course record is chaseable but not easy.** A +3 should shoot around par most days, threaten 65 on a hot day, and occasionally get humbled.
- **It plays differently every round.** Wind direction, pin positions, and Mas's form change the answers.
- **It grows.** The design system supports more courses later — same par structure, different land.
