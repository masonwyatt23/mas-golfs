# Wyatt National — Course Design v2

**Par 72 · 7,214 yards · Staunton, Virginia.** A championship parkland course in the Blue Ridge foothills, designed in the strategic school — every hole offers a choice, and the bold line is always rewarded with a better angle, never merely punished. Two loops of nine (MacKenzie's first principle), each par 36, each with two par 3s, two par 5s, and five par 4s. Four par 3s, four par 5s, ten par 4s in total.

> v2 keeps the v1 routing, pars, and yardages. What changes is the detail: every hole now has a stated question, an ideal line, a named penalty for the wrong miss, a green-complex note, and wind/elevation notes — the information the sim's decision engine and the broadcast commentary actually need.

## Design philosophy — what the research says

**The strategic school (MacKenzie).** The highest form of design rewards thought, not just power: a bold line flirts with the hazard but shortens the hole or opens the green; a safe line leaves a longer or more awkward next shot. MacKenzie's thirteen principles (*Golf Architecture*, 1920) govern this course: two loops of nine; a large proportion of good two-shot holes and at least four one-shot holes; every hole different in character; minimum blindness on approaches; artificial features indistinguishable from nature; heroic carries with an alternate route always open to the weaker player; infinite variety in the shots required; and greens boldly contoured — always rewarding the approach from the correct angle.

**The three schools.** Penal design says there is one right way. Strategic design offers several routes, each with its own price. Heroic design invites the player to bite off as much as they dare. Nearly every hole at Wyatt National blends all three — and the blend is stated per hole below, so the decision engine knows which school dominates.

**Minimalism (Doak, Coore & Crenshaw, Hanse).** Find the holes the land already suggests, move as little dirt as possible, and let width and contour do the strategic work instead of rough and water. Fairways are wide; the *angle* is the defense. Bunkers are placed to punish poor angles, not just miss-hit shots. Greens are firm and fast, Hanse-style, so the ground game — the run-up, the bump-and-run — is a live option on most approaches.

**Visual deception (Dye).** Used sparingly and honestly: a bunker that looks like it guards the green but actually sits 30 yards short; a fairway that looks narrower than it is; a green that looks flat from the fairway and isn't. Deception is a spice, not the meal — Dye's imitators failed by making courses difficult without being interesting.

**Template holes, adapted.** The great templates are referenced where the land earns them, never pasted: a Redan-style par 3 (No. 16), an Eden-style par 3 (No. 8), a Cape-style par 5 (No. 2), a Short par 3 (No. 3), and a Road Hole–flavored par 4 (No. 17). The sim treats each template as a known strategic pattern, not a copy.

**Wind and elevation as design features.** The routing is built around the prevailing southwest wind: holes run in every compass direction so no two consecutive holes play the same wind, and the wind turns a benign hole into a monster depending on the day. Elevation is used for strategy, not scenery — uphill holes play long and demand knockdowns; downhill holes invite the high ball and punish the long miss.

**Green complexes.** Every green is different in size, shape, tilt, and tiering (MacKenzie's rule: the green must be different from every other green on the course). Greens slope up to the back at least 1.3% to help hold shots, are set for visibility from the main landing zones, and are defended on the side the approach comes from. The safe miss is always short or front — never long, never above the hole. Shaved run-off areas around several greens test short-game creativity instead of thick rough.

## Front nine — out (par 36, 3,612 yards)

### 1. The Handshake — Par 4, 412 yards, downhill
- **The question:** How aggressive do you want to be on the very first swing?
- **Ideal line:** Driver down the left-center, 265–275 yards, leaving a 9-iron in. The fairway is the widest on the course (42 yards) — width is the welcome.
- **Wrong miss:** Long of the green. The green is narrow (22 yards deep) and falls away at the back; anything long is a near-certain bogey from a downhill lie.
- **Green complex:** Two-tier, back tier 18 inches higher, sloping back-to-front at 2%. Front pin is a birdie look; back pin demands the approach land short and release.
- **Wind/elevation:** 25 feet downhill from tee to green; plays ~395. Prevailing wind is helping and left-to-right — the drive drifts toward the right rough if not shaped.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** none — a pure opener.

### 2. The Reach — Par 5, 578 yards, dogleg left around a lake
- **The question:** Do you cut the corner over the water, or play it as a three-shot hole?
- **Ideal line:** A high draw over the lake's corner, carrying 265 yards of water-adjacent fairway, leaves 235 yards in — reachable with a 3-wood. The bold line also opens the green, which is angled away from the safe route.
- **Wrong miss:** Short-right into the lake on the tee shot, or long-left into the pines on the second. The safe bail right leaves a blind third shot over a cross-bunker.
- **Green complex:** Large, 34 yards wide, tilted hard left-to-right toward the water. A draw into the green holds; a fade runs toward the lake. Front bunker is deep and flashed — a lie you cannot spin from.
- **Wind/elevation:** Slightly downhill to the lake, then flat. Into the wind it is a three-shot hole for everyone; downwind the eagle putt is real. The lake makes the wind visible — ripples tell Mas the strength before he swings.
- **School:** Heroic (Cape template). **Inspiration:** Augusta 13th (Azalea) — the draw off the tee, the creek along the hole, the eagle that is always one good swing away.

### 3. The Postage Stamp — Par 3, 187 yards, uphill
- **The question:** Can you hit a small target with a mid-iron and no bail-out?
- **Ideal line:** A high 7-iron to the front-center, landing soft. The green is tiny (5,800 sq ft) and the only safe landing is the front half.
- **Wrong miss:** Long — the green falls off a false front into a collection hollow; short — the deep front bunker, 7 feet below the putting surface, with a steep flashed face.
- **Green complex:** A Short-hole template: small, crowned, sloping hard back-to-front at 3%, with a thumbprint hollow on the left. Pins on the crown are nearly unholdable; pins in the bowl are birdie chances. The green repels the imperfect strike.
- **Wind/elevation:** 15 feet uphill; plays ~195. Sheltered by pines, so the wind is unreadable from the tee — Mas must read it from the flag and the treetops.
- **School:** Penal, softened by a generous front bail-out short of the bunker. **Template:** Short.

### 4. The Examination — Par 4, 448 yards, uphill into the wind
- **The question:** Can you hit two genuinely good shots into the wind?
- **Ideal line:** A low, piercing drive at the left-center bunker — carry it (250 yards) and the ball kicks forward down the slope; lay up short of it and the approach is 200 yards uphill.
- **Wrong miss:** Right rough. The fairway tilts right-to-left, and the right rough is the thickest on the course; from it the green is blind behind a ridge.
- **Green complex:** The largest on the course (7,400 sq ft), three sections separated by a spine. A long approach that lands on the wrong section leaves a 60-foot putt over the spine. The green slopes up to the back at 1.5% — it holds a well-struck long iron and rejects a thin one.
- **Wind/elevation:** 40 feet uphill, straight into the prevailing wind. Plays 470+ on a still day, 490+ into a breeze. This is the hole where Mas clubs up and hits the knockdown — the broadcast's signature shot.
- **School:** Strategic, leaning penal. **Template:** none.

### 5. The Temptation — Par 4, 376 yards, drivable in the right wind
- **The question:** Driver at the green, or iron to the fat of the fairway?
- **Ideal line:** Driver, high and soft, carrying the centerline bunker at 290 yards and landing on the front apron. In a helping wind the green is reachable; into the wind it is not, and the correct play is a 5-iron to 120 yards.
- **Wrong miss:** The centerline bunker itself — a 12-foot-deep pot bunker, Dye-style, from which the only play is out sideways. Or long, over a green that sits on a plateau with fall-offs on three sides.
- **Green complex:** Small (5,200 sq ft), firm, sloping hard front-to-back — the opposite of most greens here. Approaches must land short and run on; aerial attacks bounce through. A shaved run-off left invites the bump-and-run.
- **Wind/elevation:** Flat, but the most wind-exposed tee on the course — no trees, so the wind is honest and readable. Downwind it is the easiest birdie on the course; into the wind it plays like a 400-yard par 4.
- **School:** Heroic. **Inspiration:** the driveable par 4s of the great links — the decision, not the distance, is the test.

### 6. The Dogleg — Par 4, 431 yards, hard right around pines
- **The question:** Draw it around the corner, or play the long way around?
- **Ideal line:** A draw starting at the pines and bending 25 yards, cutting the corner and leaving 95 yards in. The draw also matches the fairway's left-to-right tilt, so the ball kicks forward.
- **Wrong miss:** Straight — through the fairway into the pines, where the only play is a punch-out. Or over-drawn — into the left rough, from which the green is blind behind the corner trees.
- **Green complex:** Angled 30 degrees to the fairway, open on the left, guarded by a deep bunker on the right. The correct angle (from the corner) sees the whole green; the safe angle (from the right) sees only a sliver. MacKenzie's rule in pure form: the approach rewards the correct angle.
- **Wind/elevation:** Downhill 20 feet, then flat. The prevailing wind is across, right-to-left — it helps the draw and punishes the fade.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** none.

### 7. The Valley — Par 5, 552 yards, downhill tee, uphill second
- **The question:** What is your lay-up number?
- **Ideal line:** Driver down the valley floor, then a lay-up to exactly 100 yards — the number Mas's wedge proximity is elite from. The cross-bunker at 90 yards short of the green catches the greedy lay-up that tries to get closer.
- **Wrong miss:** A second shot that chases the green and finds the cross-bunker, or a lay-up that goes past 100 yards into the bunker. The green is small and the approach must be a full wedge, not a half shot.
- **Green complex:** Two-tier with a 2-foot step, the back tier sloping away. Front pin: attack. Back pin: land it short of the step and let it release — anything long is off the back into a hollow.
- **Wind/elevation:** 35 feet down off the tee, 30 feet up on the approach. The valley funnels the wind, so it gusts — Mas must wait for a lull or flight it low.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** none.

### 8. The Long One — Par 3, 226 yards, over water, into the wind
- **The question:** Can you commit to a long iron over water when the only safe miss is short?
- **Ideal line:** A high hybrid or 4-iron, landing on the front third and releasing to the middle. The green is wide (36 yards) but shallow (24 yards deep) — distance control is everything.
- **Wrong miss:** Long — water behind the green as well as in front (the pond wraps the back-left). Short — the pond. The Eden template's lesson: the safe miss is short, in the front bunker, not in the water.
- **Green complex:** An Eden-style green: wide, shallow, with a deep pot bunker front-right and a hollow back-left. The putting surface slopes gently toward the water — putts break toward the pond, a visual deception Dye would admire.
- **Wind/elevation:** Flat, directly into the prevailing wind, fully exposed. Plays 240+ into a breeze. The wind is the defense — on a calm day it is a mid-iron; into the wind it is the hardest par 3 on the course.
- **School:** Heroic, softened by the front bunker as a save. **Template:** Eden. **Inspiration:** Augusta 12th (Golden Bell) — the swirling wind, the narrow target, the water that makes the heart race.

### 9. The Return — Par 4, 402 yards, downhill to the clubhouse
- **The question:** Can you finish the nine with a scoring hole?
- **Ideal line:** Driver at the front-right bunker, fading it off the slope, leaving 110 yards in. The fairway is generous; the hole rewards a confident swing.
- **Wrong miss:** Left rough, from which the green — tucked behind that front-right bunker — is blind. Long is the clubhouse terrace; the gallery sees everything.
- **Green complex:** Wide and shallow, sloping back-to-front at 2.5%, with a false front. The front-right bunker is the only guard; the green is otherwise open, inviting the attack. Back pin is the Sunday pin — 6 feet from the back edge, with the fall-off behind.
- **Wind/elevation:** 30 feet downhill; plays ~385. Helping wind. The easiest approach on the front nine — a birdie hole that sets up the turn.
- **School:** Strategic, gentle. **Template:** none.

## Back nine — in (par 36, 3,602 yards)

### 10. The Turn — Par 4, 419 yards, elevated tee, dogleg left
- **The question:** Can you hold the left side of a fairway that wants to feed everything right?
- **Ideal line:** A fade off the elevated tee, landing on the left half. The fairway tilts right-to-left on the landing area, so a draw runs into the right rough; the fade holds the line.
- **Wrong miss:** Right rough — thick, downhill lie, approach to a green that is open only from the left. The approach from the right sees a bunker and a narrow sliver.
- **Green complex:** Long and narrow (38 yards deep, 20 wide), tilted toward the fairway, with a spine down the middle. Front pin is accessible; back pin requires carrying the spine. The green sits in a natural amphitheater, so the gallery (and the cameras) see the whole approach.
- **Wind/elevation:** 25 feet down from the tee, then flat. The elevated tee makes the wind readable — the flag is visible from the tee box, a MacKenzie visibility rule.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** none.

### 11. The Monster — Par 5, 591 yards, uphill into the wind, all the way
- **The question:** Can you make three genuinely good shots in a row?
- **Ideal line:** Driver, then a 3-wood to the left side of the fairway (avoiding the bunkers at 300 yards on the second shot), then a mid-iron in. There is no shortcut — the hole is a test of execution, not decision.
- **Wrong miss:** The fairway bunkers on the second shot — two deep, flashed bunkers that cost a full stroke. Or the right rough off the tee, from which the second shot is blind over a ridge.
- **Green complex:** The most heavily defended on the course: bunkers front-left, front-right, and long. The green is large but the entrance is narrow — the approach must be precise, not just long. It slopes up to the back at 1.3%, holding a well-struck iron.
- **Wind/elevation:** 55 feet uphill overall, straight into the prevailing wind. Plays 620+ into a breeze. The longest hole on the course, and the one where Mas's +3 shows — three good shots is par; anything less is a bogey.
- **School:** Penal, honestly so — the one hole where length and execution are the test. **Template:** none.

### 12. The Picture — Par 3, 164 yards, downhill over a pond
- **The question:** Can you enjoy the prettiest hole on the course and still hit the shot?
- **Ideal line:** A smooth wedge, high and soft, to the middle of the green. The hole is short; the test is the mind, not the yardage.
- **Wrong miss:** Short — the pond, which fronts the entire green. Long — a bunker behind, from which the putt runs back toward the water. The green is framed by pines, and the beauty is the hazard: the view pulls the eye up and the swing with it.
- **Green complex:** Kidney-shaped, 28 yards wide, sloping gently toward the water. Two pin zones: front-left (near the water, the Sunday pin) and back-right (safe, the everyday pin). The putting surface is the flattest on the course — the hole is won or lost on the tee shot.
- **Wind/elevation:** 20 feet downhill; plays ~150. Sheltered, so the wind is light — but the pond's surface shows every breath of it.
- **School:** Heroic, in miniature. **Template:** none — the signature hole. **Inspiration:** the 17th at Sawgrass in spirit (water, nerves, a small target) at a friendlier scale.

### 13. The Grind — Par 4, 455 yards, narrow, uphill, the hardest par 4
- **The question:** Can you hit the fairway and then hold a green that slopes away from you?
- **Ideal line:** A low, drawing drive between the bunkers (240 yards, both sides), then a long iron or hybrid to the front of the green. The approach must land short — the green slopes away, so anything that lands on the putting surface runs through.
- **Wrong miss:** Either fairway bunker — both are deep, and from them the green is unreachable in regulation. Long of the green is a collection hollow with a downhill lie.
- **Green complex:** The most severe on the course: a two-tier green sloping away from the fairway at 2%, with a false front and a run-off long. The only way to hold it is to land the approach on the front apron and let it run on — the ground game, Hanse-style, is the correct play.
- **Wind/elevation:** 45 feet uphill, into the wind. Plays 480+. The hardest par 4 on the course, and the stroke index 1.
- **School:** Penal, softened by the run-up option. **Template:** none.

### 14. The Gambler — Par 4, 388 yards, creek crossing at 280
- **The question:** Carry the creek or lay back?
- **Ideal line:** Driver over the creek (carry 285 yards) leaves a flip wedge in — but only if the wind is helping or calm. Into the wind, the correct play is a 5-iron to 130 yards, short of the creek.
- **Wrong miss:** The creek, obviously — but the subtler miss is the lay-up that runs through the fairway into the creek from the other side. The creek crosses diagonally, so the lay-up line matters as much as the carry.
- **Green complex:** Small and firm, sloping front-to-back, with a shaved run-off on the left. The approach from 60 yards must be a full, committed wedge — a tentative one spins back off the front.
- **Wind/elevation:** Flat. The creek makes the wind visible and the decision binary — the hole's drama is entirely in the tee-shot choice.
- **School:** Heroic. **Template:** none.

### 15. The Eagle Chance — Par 5, 545 yards, reachable in two downwind
- **The question:** Go for the green in two, or lay up to your number?
- **Ideal line:** Driver, then a high 3-wood or hybrid to the green — but only downwind. The green is guarded by a bunker complex in front; the safe play is long and left, where a run-off feeds the ball back toward the hole.
- **Wrong miss:** The front bunker complex — three deep bunkers that make up-and-down a 50/50 proposition. Short-right is the worst miss: a hollow from which the green is blind.
- **Green complex:** Large, 36 yards wide, with a back-left bowl that collects long approaches and feeds them toward the hole — the reward for the bold second shot. Front pins are guarded; back pins are accessible.
- **Wind/elevation:** Slightly downhill, usually downwind — the prevailing wind helps on this hole by design, so the eagle chance is real most days. Into the wind it becomes a three-shot hole, and the lay-up number (100 yards) is the play.
- **School:** Heroic. **Inspiration:** Augusta 13th again, from the other direction — the second shot is the hole.

### 16. The Test — Par 3, 203 yards, two-tier green, deep front bunker
- **The question:** Can you hit the correct tier from 200 yards?
- **Ideal line:** A mid-iron to the correct tier — front pin, land it short and let it release; back pin, carry it all the way to the top tier. The wrong tier is a three-putt waiting to happen.
- **Wrong miss:** The deep front bunker — 8 feet below the putting surface, with a flashed face. Or the wrong tier, which is a miss in everything but name.
- **Green complex:** A Redan-style green: tilted from front-right to back-left, falling away on the left, guarded by the deep bunker on the right. The classic Redan lesson applies — the shot that lands short-right and runs on is the correct play; the shot that flies at the flag is the sucker play.
- **Wind/elevation:** Flat, across the prevailing wind (left-to-right). The wind pushes the ball toward the bunker — Mas must start it left of the flag and let the wind bring it back.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** Redan.

### 17. The Penultimate — Par 4, 427 yards, dogleg right, out of bounds on the corner
- **The question:** How much of the corner do you dare cut?
- **Ideal line:** A fade that starts at the corner and bends back to the fairway, cutting 30 yards off the hole. The fade also matches the out-of-bounds on the right — the shot shape and the hazard are in tension, which is the point.
- **Wrong miss:** Over the corner — out of bounds, stroke and distance. Or through the fairway into the left trees, where the only play is a punch-out (the Road Hole's lesson: the recovery shot is part of the hole).
- **Green complex:** Narrow, 20 yards wide, guarded by the Road Hole bunker — a deep, revetted pot bunker on the front-left, from which the only play is out sideways. A stone wall lines the back of the green; anything long is a penalty.
- **Wind/elevation:** Slightly uphill, across the wind. The tee shot is blind over the corner — the one blind tee shot on the course, earned by the hole's character (MacKenzie allows blindness on the tee shot, never on the approach).
- **School:** Heroic with a penal edge. **Template:** Road Hole. **Inspiration:** the 17th at St. Andrews — the hardest hole in the world, at Wyatt National scale.

### 18. The Closer — Par 4, 410 yards, uphill to a clubhouse green
- **The question:** Can you finish in front of everyone?
- **Ideal line:** Driver at the fairway bunker at 300 yards — carry it and the ball kicks forward down the slope; lay up short and the approach is 170 uphill.
- **Wrong miss:** The fairway bunker (deep, flashed, at driving distance — the last test). Or long of the green, where the gallery and the terrace see the recovery.
- **Green complex:** Wide and welcoming, sloping back-to-front at 2%, with a false front and a back tier. The green is the largest on the back nine — a fitting stage. Front pins are birdie looks; back pins demand the approach land short and release.
- **Wind/elevation:** 30 feet uphill to a clubhouse green, with the gallery behind it. Helping wind. The approach is hit toward the people — the broadcast's closing shot.
- **School:** Strategic. **Template:** none.

## Routing notes

- **Two loops of nine** (MacKenzie principle 1): both nines start and end at the clubhouse. No long walks between green and tee.
- **Wind rotation:** holes run in every compass direction; no two consecutive holes play the same wind. The prevailing southwest wind helps on 1, 5, 9, 15, and 18; hurts on 4, 8, 11, and 13; crosses on 6, 10, 16, and 17.
- **Elevation rhythm:** the course climbs and falls in waves — downhill opener, uphill examination, valley par 5, downhill signature par 3, uphill grinder, uphill closer. No hill climbing for its own sake (MacKenzie principle 4): every climb is a strategic feature.
- **Difficulty arc:** the front nine opens gently and hardens (4, 8); the back nine opens with a test (10, 11), breathes at the signature par 3 (12), grinds (13), gambles (14, 15), tests (16, 17), and closes with a scoring chance (18). The stroke index order: 13, 4, 11, 17, 8, 6, 2, 16, 10, 14, 7, 1, 15, 3, 5, 9, 18, 12.
- **Water in play on six holes:** 2 (lake), 8 (pond), 12 (pond), 14 (creek), 15 (hollow — no water, but the bunker complex plays like one), 17 (no water — the wall and the bunker are the hazard). Water is functional, never decorative: it always guards the bold line, never the safe one.

## What makes it great (v2 additions)

- **Every hole asks a question, and the question is now written down.** The decision engine and the commentary both work from the same per-hole brief.
- **The miss is designed, not accidental.** Every green has a named safe miss and a named penalty miss, and the bunkers punish the wrong angle, not just the miss-hit shot.
- **The ground game is alive.** Firm, fast greens with run-up options on 5, 9, 13, and 15 mean the correct play is sometimes a bump-and-run, not a flop — and the broadcast can teach that.
- **The course record is chaseable but not easy.** A +3 should shoot around par most days, threaten 65 on a hot day, and occasionally get humbled — and the Road to 59 chase is credible precisely because 59 requires a near-perfect day, not a normal one.
- **It plays differently every round.** Wind direction, pin positions, and Mas's form change the answers — the same hole asks a different question on a different day.
