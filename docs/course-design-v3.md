# Wyatt National — Course Design v3 (Championship Length)

**Par 72 · 7,602 yards · Staunton, Virginia.** The same course Mason knows — same routing, same names, same template holes, same strategic school — stretched to a real championship length. v2 was 7,214 yards; v3 is **7,602 yards (+388)**, front nine 3,808, back nine 3,794. The extra length goes where a championship course earns it: the par 5s (+134 across four holes), the long par 4s (+214 across ten), and a little on the par 3s (+40 across four) so no one-shot hole becomes a wedge.

> v3 keeps the v2 routing, pars, names, azimuths, templates, and wind rotation. What changes is the length, the land shaping, and the green complexes — the three things Mason asked for: more slope, rounded fairway edges blending into rough, and greens with real slope and contour. The identity holes are untouched in character: the drivable 5th, the reachable 12th "The Picture" over the pond, the 17th Road Hole, the 18th closer.

## What v3 changes, and why

**Length (+388 yards).** A 7,214-yard course is a strong club course. A 7,602-yard course is a championship venue — the length the broadcast's "Road to 59" chase needs to stay credible, and the length that makes a +3 golfer's 265–275-yard driving actually matter. The added yards are not spread evenly: the four par 5s take the most (+33 to +34 each) because a championship par 5 should demand three committed shots or a genuinely heroic second; the hardest par 4s take the next most (4th +28, 13th +27); the drivable 5th takes the least (+16) so it stays drivable; the signature 12th takes the least of all (+8) so it stays a wedge over water.

**Slope — the land moves more.** v2 already used elevation; v3 uses it harder. Every hole now states its elevation change and its fairway camber (the lateral tilt that feeds balls toward one side). The climbs get longer and steeper where the hole is meant to be hard (4th, 11th, 13th), the downhill holes get more drop (1st, 9th, 12th), and no fairway is flat side-to-side anymore — a flat fairway is a boring fairway. Greens slope harder too: the v2 minimum of 1.3% back-to-front becomes a floor, not a target, and the severe greens (13th, 16th) push toward 2.5–3%.

**Rounded fairway edges.** This is the single biggest visual and strategic change. In v1/v2 the fairway was a corridor with a hard edge: inside the width you were on short grass, outside it you were in rough. In v3 every fairway edge is rounded and scalloped — the mow line wanders, the fairway bulges toward the bold line and pinches toward the hazard, and a **first cut of semi-rough (6–8 yards wide)** blends the fairway into the deep rough. There is no hard line to aim at or miss by an inch; there is a gradient. The strategic consequence: the bold line is rewarded with a wider landing area, the safe line is punished with a narrower one — the fairway itself is now part of the question. The sim consequence is specified in §5: the lie model gains a semi-rough band.

**Bunkers as landforms, not stickers.** Bunkers in v3 sit in the ground, not on it: flashed sand faces on the fairway bunkers, revetted pot faces on the 5th and the 17th Road Hole bunker, grass-faced hollows on the greenside complexes. Driving-distance bunkers stay at driving distance (270–300 yards) because Mas's driving distance does not change; greenside bunkers move outward with the greens. One new bunker is added (a second fairway bunker on the lengthened 11th) and one is reshaped (the 7th cross-bunker moves to stay 90 yards short of the green).

**Greens with slope and contour.** Every green in v3 states its size, its overall slope, its tier structure, and its contour character. The rule from v2 stands — no two greens alike — and the new rule is: **the green must be readable from the fairway and unreadable from the tee.** A player who has played the hole knows the slope; a player who hasn't is guessing. Putts break toward the water on 8 and 12, away from the fall-off on 5 and 13, and over a spine on 4 and 10.

## Design philosophy — unchanged from v2, applied harder

**The strategic school (MacKenzie).** Same thirteen principles, same blend of strategic/heroic/penal per hole. Lengthening a hole must never turn a strategic hole into a penal one: every added yard is placed where it lengthens the *approach*, not where it removes the *choice*. The 5th stays a decision, the 14th stays a gamble, the 17th stays a dare.

**Minimalism (Doak, Coore & Crenshaw, Hanse).** The extra length comes from new back tees set into the existing land — a tee pushed 30 yards into a natural hollow, a tee moved to the far side of a ridge — not from bulldozed new corridors. Width and contour still do the strategic work instead of rough and water.

**Visual deception (Dye).** Used sparingly and honestly, as in v2. The lengthened holes add one deception each: the 4th's new back tee makes the fairway look narrower than it is; the 11th's second fairway bunker looks greenside from the second-shot landing area and is actually 300 yards out.

**Template holes, adapted.** Unchanged from v2: Short (3), Cape (2), Eden (8), Redan (16), Road Hole (17). The templates are the course's memory — v3 does not touch them.

## Front nine — out (par 36, 3,808 yards)

### 1. The Handshake — Par 4, 432 yards, downhill (was 412)
- **The question:** How aggressive do you want to be on the very first swing?
- **Slope/elevation:** 30 feet downhill from tee to green (was 25 — the new back tee sits higher on the ridge). Plays ~410. Fairway cambers gently left-to-right, feeding the drive toward the right rough; the left side is the high side and the correct side.
- **Fairway shape:** The widest on the course (44 yards at the landing area), edges rounded and scalloped, a 7-yard semi-rough band blending into the rough on both sides. The fairway bulges toward the left-center — the bold line gets the widest target.
- **Bunkers:** Two, as in v2, rescaled — a fairway bunker left at 290 (driving distance, unchanged) and a greenside bunker right at 405. Both flashed sand faces.
- **Green:** Two-tier, back tier 18 inches higher, sloping back-to-front at 2%. 22 yards deep, narrow. Front pin is a birdie look; back pin demands the approach land short and release. The miss is long, as ever — the green falls away at the back.
- **Wind:** Helping, left-to-right. **School:** Strategic.

### 2. The Reach — Par 5, 612 yards, dogleg left around a lake (was 578)
- **The question:** Do you cut the corner over the water, or play it as a three-shot hole?
- **Slope/elevation:** Slightly downhill to the lake, then flat. The new back tee adds 34 yards of flat carry before the corner — the decision point is unchanged, the second shot is 34 yards longer.
- **Fairway shape:** 40 yards wide, dogleg left at 280 (unchanged corner). The fairway's left edge rounds hard around the lake — the mow line follows the water's edge, so the bold line is visually a narrower ribbon of grass hugging the hazard. Semi-rough band 8 yards on the safe (right) side only; the lake side has no semi-rough, just water.
- **Bunkers:** One fairway bunker right at 310 (unchanged), one deep greenside bunker front, flashed face, 7 feet below the putting surface.
- **Green:** Large, 34 yards wide, tilted hard left-to-right toward the water. A draw into the green holds; a fade runs toward the lake. Front bunker is the deep flashed one — a lie you cannot spin from.
- **Wind:** Into the wind it is a three-shot hole for everyone; downwind the eagle putt is real. **School:** Heroic (Cape template). **Inspiration:** Augusta 13th.

### 3. The Postage Stamp — Par 3, 196 yards, uphill (was 187)
- **The question:** Can you hit a small target with a mid-iron and no bail-out?
- **Slope/elevation:** 18 feet uphill (was 15); plays ~205. The green sits on a crown — the putting surface is the highest point of the hole, so the approach is always hit slightly blind over the front bunker.
- **Fairway shape:** No fairway — a tee shot hole. The surround is a rounded collar of semi-rough blending into a collection hollow long and a deep front bunker short. The green's edge is a soft, scalloped fringe, not a hard collar line.
- **Bunkers:** Deep front bunker, 7 feet below the putting surface, steep flashed face (unchanged); a smaller pot bunker back-left at 190.
- **Green:** A Short-hole template: tiny (5,800 sq ft), crowned, sloping hard back-to-front at 3%, with a thumbprint hollow on the left. Pins on the crown are nearly unholdable; pins in the bowl are birdie chances. The green repels the imperfect strike.
- **Wind:** Sheltered by pines — unreadable from the tee, read it from the flag and the treetops. **School:** Penal, softened by a generous front bail-out. **Template:** Short.

### 4. The Examination — Par 4, 476 yards, uphill into the wind (was 448)
- **The question:** Can you hit two genuinely good shots into the wind?
- **Slope/elevation:** 48 feet uphill (was 40 — the new back tee is lower, the green is higher). Plays 500+ on a still day, 520+ into a breeze. The fairway climbs in two shelves: a flat landing area, then a steady 4% rise to the green.
- **Fairway shape:** 34 yards wide, narrowing to 28 at the 270-yard bunkers. Fairway camber tilts right-to-left — the right rough is the thickest on the course and the fairway feeds toward it, so the correct drive starts at the right bunker and draws back. Edges rounded, semi-rough 7 yards.
- **Bunkers:** Left and right fairway bunkers at 270 (driving distance, unchanged), both deep and flashed; one greenside bunker left at 448. **New deception:** from the new back tee the fairway looks 10 yards narrower than it is — the bunkers' flashed faces catch the eye and shrink the target.
- **Green:** The largest on the course (7,400 sq ft), three sections separated by a spine, sloping up to the back at 1.5%. A long approach on the wrong section leaves a 60-foot putt over the spine. It holds a well-struck long iron and rejects a thin one.
- **Wind:** Straight into the prevailing wind — the knockdown hole, the broadcast's signature shot. **School:** Strategic, leaning penal.

### 5. The Temptation — Par 4, 392 yards, drivable in the right wind (was 376)
- **The question:** Driver at the green, or iron to the fat of the fairway?
- **Slope/elevation:** Flat. The green sits on a plateau with fall-offs on three sides — the plateau is the hole's defense, and the extra 16 yards makes the plateau 16 yards further from the tee, which is exactly the point: the carry is now 306 yards to the front edge, still drivable downwind, no longer drivable into the wind.
- **Fairway shape:** 36 yards wide, the widest landing area on a short par 4. The centerline bunker splits the fairway visually into two corridors; the fairway edges round away from the bunker, so the safe corridor (right) is wider and the bold corridor (left, at the green) is narrower.
- **Bunkers:** The centerline pot bunker at 290 (unchanged — Dye-style, 12 feet deep, revetted face, the only play is out sideways) and a greenside bunker left at 366.
- **Green:** Small (5,200 sq ft), firm, sloping hard **front-to-back** — the opposite of most greens here, and the reason aerial attacks bounce through. Approaches must land short and run on. A shaved run-off left invites the bump-and-run. The green's back edge is a hard fall-off; the front edge is a soft apron.
- **Wind:** The most wind-exposed tee on the course — no trees, honest and readable. Downwind it is the easiest birdie on the course; into the wind it plays like a 415-yard par 4. **School:** Heroic. **Identity preserved:** still the drivable 5th.

### 6. The Dogleg — Par 4, 452 yards, hard right around pines (was 431)
- **The question:** Draw it around the corner, or play the long way around?
- **Slope/elevation:** Downhill 22 feet, then flat. The new back tee is 21 yards further back and 6 feet higher, so the corner is more visible and the draw is more tempting.
- **Fairway shape:** 34 yards wide, dogleg right at 240 (unchanged corner). The fairway tilts left-to-right, matching the draw — the ball kicks forward off the tilt. The left edge rounds into the pines with a wide semi-rough band (10 yards) that forgives a slight over-draw; the right edge pinches to 5 yards of semi-rough before the pines.
- **Bunkers:** One deep greenside bunker right at 425, guarding the sliver of green visible from the safe angle. The corner trees are the hazard off the tee, not a bunker.
- **Green:** Angled 30 degrees to the fairway, open on the left, guarded by the deep bunker on the right. The correct angle (from the corner) sees the whole green; the safe angle (from the right) sees only a sliver. Slopes gently back-to-front at 1.5%, with a back-left bowl that collects a well-judged draw.
- **Wind:** Across, right-to-left — helps the draw, punishes the fade. **School:** Strategic.

### 7. The Valley — Par 5, 586 yards, downhill tee, uphill second (was 552)
- **The question:** What is your lay-up number?
- **Slope/elevation:** 38 feet down off the tee (was 35), 32 feet up on the approach (was 30). The valley is deeper in v3 — the new back tee sits on the far ridge, so the drive drops further and the second shot climbs longer.
- **Fairway shape:** 38 yards wide on the valley floor, narrowing to 30 at the cross-bunker. The fairway follows the valley's natural bowl — its edges are the valley walls themselves, rounded and grassy, with semi-rough climbing the slopes. A drive that misses the floor rolls back toward it; a drive that misses wide stays on the slope.
- **Bunkers:** The cross-bunker moves to stay 90 yards short of the green — now at **496 yards** (was 462). One greenside bunker left at 556. The cross-bunker is wide and shallow, a visual wall rather than a deep trap: it catches the greedy lay-up, it does not bury it.
- **Green:** Two-tier with a 2-foot step, the back tier sloping away. Front pin: attack. Back pin: land it short of the step and let it release — anything long is off the back into a hollow. The lay-up number is still 100 yards — Mas's elite wedge number — and the extra length is all in the first two shots, not the third.
- **Wind:** The valley funnels the wind, so it gusts — wait for a lull or flight it low. **School:** Strategic.

### 8. The Long One — Par 3, 238 yards, over water, into the wind (was 226)
- **The question:** Can you commit to a long iron over water when the only safe miss is short?
- **Slope/elevation:** Flat. The pond fronts the green and wraps the back-left, as in v2. The extra 12 yards is all carry over water — the tee is pushed back, the green is not moved.
- **Fairway shape:** No fairway. The green's surround is a rounded collar with a deep pot bunker front-right and a hollow back-left. The collar is wide on the short side (the safe miss) and narrow on the long side.
- **Bunkers:** Deep pot bunker front-right, 6 feet below the surface; a smaller bunker back-left at 232.
- **Green:** An Eden-style green: wide (36 yards), shallow (24 yards deep), sloping gently **toward the water** — putts break toward the pond, a visual deception Dye would admire. Distance control is everything; the green is shallow enough that a 5-yard miss in distance is a bunker or a hollow.
- **Wind:** Flat, directly into the prevailing wind, fully exposed. Plays 250+ into a breeze. The wind is the defense — on a calm day it is a mid-iron; into the wind it is the hardest par 3 on the course. **School:** Heroic, softened by the front bunker as a save. **Template:** Eden. **Inspiration:** Augusta 12th.

### 9. The Return — Par 4, 424 yards, downhill to the clubhouse (was 402)
- **The question:** Can you finish the nine with a scoring hole?
- **Slope/elevation:** 34 feet downhill (was 30); plays ~400. The fairway drops in one long slope, then flattens for the last 80 yards — the approach is hit from flat ground to a green that sits slightly above the fairway.
- **Fairway shape:** Generous, 40 yards wide, edges rounded and wide — the friendliest fairway on the front nine. The fairway bulges toward the front-right bunker (the aiming point) and pinches on the left, where the blind rough waits.
- **Bunkers:** Front-right bunker at 397 (unchanged position relative to the green), one greenside bunker left at 405.
- **Green:** Wide and shallow, sloping back-to-front at 2.5%, with a false front. The front-right bunker is the only guard; the green is otherwise open, inviting the attack. Back pin is the Sunday pin — 6 feet from the back edge, with the fall-off behind.
- **Wind:** Helping. The easiest approach on the front nine — a birdie hole that sets up the turn. **School:** Strategic, gentle.

## Back nine — in (par 36, 3,794 yards)

### 10. The Turn — Par 4, 440 yards, elevated tee, dogleg left (was 419)
- **The question:** Can you hold the left side of a fairway that wants to feed everything right?
- **Slope/elevation:** 28 feet down from the tee (was 25), then flat. The elevated tee makes the wind readable — the flag is visible from the tee box, a MacKenzie visibility rule.
- **Fairway shape:** 36 yards wide, dogleg left at 230 (unchanged corner). The fairway tilts hard right-to-left on the landing area — a draw runs into the right rough, a fade holds the line. The left edge is rounded and wide (10 yards of semi-rough); the right edge pinches to 4 yards before the thick rough. The fairway itself is the test: it wants the ball right, the hole wants it left.
- **Bunkers:** Fairway bunker left at 280 (unchanged), greenside bunker right at 412.
- **Green:** Long and narrow (38 yards deep, 20 wide), tilted toward the fairway, with a spine down the middle. Front pin is accessible; back pin requires carrying the spine. The green sits in a natural amphitheater, so the gallery — and the cameras — see the whole approach. Slopes back-to-front at 1.8%.
- **Wind:** Helping off the elevated tee. **School:** Strategic.

### 11. The Monster — Par 5, 624 yards, uphill into the wind, all the way (was 591)
- **The question:** Can you make three genuinely good shots in a row?
- **Slope/elevation:** 60 feet uphill overall (was 55). The hole climbs in three shelves — the drive climbs 20, the second climbs 20, the approach climbs 20. No flat stretch longer than 100 yards.
- **Fairway shape:** 34 yards wide, narrowing to 30 at the second-shot bunkers. The fairway cambers left-to-right on the first shelf and right-to-left on the second, so the correct shape changes with each shot — a draw off the tee, a fade on the second. Edges rounded, semi-rough 7 yards.
- **Bunkers:** Two fairway bunkers at 300 on the second shot (unchanged — the hole's defining hazard), **one new fairway bunker left at 340** (the added length creates a new landing area for the second shot, and it needs a guard), and a greenside bunker left at 594. The green is the most heavily defended on the course: bunkers front-left, front-right, and long, with a narrow entrance.
- **Green:** Large but the entrance is narrow — the approach must be precise, not just long. Slopes up to the back at 1.3%, holding a well-struck iron. A back-left bowl collects a long approach that misses left.
- **Wind:** Straight into the prevailing wind. Plays 650+ into a breeze. The longest hole on the course, and the one where Mas's +3 shows — three good shots is par; anything less is a bogey. **School:** Penal, honestly so — the one hole where length and execution are the test.

### 12. The Picture — Par 3, 172 yards, downhill over a pond (was 164)
- **The question:** Can you enjoy the prettiest hole on the course and still hit the shot?
- **Slope/elevation:** 22 feet downhill (was 20); plays ~156. The pond fronts the entire green. The extra 8 yards is tee yardage only — the green, the pond, and the framing pines do not move. This hole is not lengthened to be harder; it is lengthened so the tee sits with the other championship tees.
- **Fairway shape:** No fairway. The green is framed by pines on both sides, and the surround is a rounded collar that falls toward the water on the front and into a bunker on the back.
- **Bunkers:** One bunker behind the green at 166 — from it, the putt runs back toward the water.
- **Green:** Kidney-shaped, 28 yards wide, sloping gently toward the water. Two pin zones: front-left (near the water, the Sunday pin) and back-right (safe, the everyday pin). The putting surface is the flattest on the course (0.8% overall) — the hole is won or lost on the tee shot.
- **Wind:** Sheltered, light — but the pond's surface shows every breath of it. **School:** Heroic, in miniature. **Identity preserved:** still the reachable signature hole — a wedge over water, the prettiest hole on the course. **Inspiration:** the 17th at Sawgrass in spirit, at a friendlier scale.

### 13. The Grind — Par 4, 482 yards, narrow, uphill, the hardest par 4 (was 455)
- **The question:** Can you hit the fairway and then hold a green that slopes away from you?
- **Slope/elevation:** 52 feet uphill (was 45). Plays 505+. The fairway climbs steadily at 3–4% with no flat stretch — the longest continuous climb on the course.
- **Fairway shape:** The narrowest on the course: 28 yards wide at the landing area, 26 at the bunkers. Edges rounded but tight — 5 yards of semi-rough, then deep rough. The fairway cambers slightly left-to-right, feeding the drive toward the right bunker.
- **Bunkers:** Left and right fairway bunkers at 275 (driving distance, unchanged), both deep; one greenside bunker left at 456. From either fairway bunker the green is unreachable in regulation.
- **Green:** The most severe on the course: a two-tier green sloping **away** from the fairway at 2%, with a false front and a run-off long. The only way to hold it is to land the approach on the front apron and let it run on — the ground game, Hanse-style, is the correct play. A putt from above the hole is a three-putt waiting to happen.
- **Wind:** Into the wind. The hardest par 4 on the course, and the stroke index 1. **School:** Penal, softened by the run-up option.

### 14. The Gambler — Par 4, 406 yards, creek crossing at 280 (was 388)
- **The question:** Carry the creek or lay back?
- **Slope/elevation:** Flat. The creek crosses diagonally at 280 (unchanged) — the diagonal is the hole's genius: the lay-up line matters as much as the carry, because a lay-up that runs through the fairway finds the creek from the other side.
- **Fairway shape:** 36 yards wide, pinching to 30 at the creek crossing. The fairway's left edge rounds toward the creek; the right edge is wide and forgiving. The creek itself is a natural hazard with rounded, grassy banks — no hard edge, no wooden revetting.
- **Bunkers:** One greenside bunker right at 380.
- **Green:** Small and firm, sloping front-to-back at 1.5%, with a shaved run-off on the left. The approach from 60 yards must be a full, committed wedge — a tentative one spins back off the front.
- **Wind:** Flat, and the creek makes the wind visible — the hole's drama is entirely in the tee-shot choice. The extra 18 yards is all past the creek: the carry decision is unchanged, the approach is 18 yards longer, and the flip wedge becomes a three-quarter wedge. **School:** Heroic.

### 15. The Eagle Chance — Par 5, 578 yards, reachable in two downwind (was 545)
- **The question:** Go for the green in two, or lay up to your number?
- **Slope/elevation:** Slightly downhill, usually downwind — the prevailing wind helps on this hole by design, so the eagle chance is real most days. The new back tee adds 33 yards of gentle downhill, so the second shot is longer but the angle is the same.
- **Fairway shape:** 38 yards wide, dogleg slightly left. The fairway's left edge rounds toward the green, opening the angle for the second shot; the right edge pinches toward the front bunker complex.
- **Bunkers:** The front bunker complex — three deep bunkers at 532, 542, and 552 — guards the green. Short-right is the worst miss: a hollow from which the green is blind.
- **Green:** Large, 36 yards wide, with a back-left bowl that collects long approaches and feeds them toward the hole — the reward for the bold second shot. Front pins are guarded; back pins are accessible. Slopes back-to-front at 1.6%.
- **Wind:** Downwind by design. Into the wind it becomes a three-shot hole, and the lay-up number (100 yards) is the play. **School:** Heroic. **Inspiration:** Augusta 13th, from the other direction — the second shot is the hole.

### 16. The Test — Par 3, 214 yards, two-tier green, deep front bunker (was 203)
- **The question:** Can you hit the correct tier from 200 yards?
- **Slope/elevation:** Flat, across the prevailing wind (left-to-right). The wind pushes the ball toward the bunker — Mas must start it left of the flag and let the wind bring it back.
- **Fairway shape:** No fairway. The surround is a rounded collar with a shaved run-off on the left (the Redan's bail-out) and the deep bunker on the right.
- **Bunkers:** The deep front bunker — 8 feet below the putting surface, flashed face — at 196; a smaller pot bunker back-left at 206.
- **Green:** A Redan-style green: tilted from front-right to back-left, falling away on the left, sloping overall at 2.5% — the steepest putting surface on the course. The classic Redan lesson applies: the shot that lands short-right and runs on is the correct play; the shot that flies at the flag is the sucker play. Two tiers separated by a 14-inch step; the wrong tier is a three-putt waiting to happen.
- **Wind:** Across, left-to-right. **School:** Strategic. **Template:** Redan.

### 17. The Penultimate — Par 4, 448 yards, dogleg right, out of bounds on the corner (was 427)
- **The question:** How much of the corner do you dare cut?
- **Slope/elevation:** Slightly uphill, 12 feet. The tee shot is blind over the corner — the one blind tee shot on the course, earned by the hole's character (MacKenzie allows blindness on the tee shot, never on the approach).
- **Fairway shape:** 32 yards wide, dogleg right at 250 (unchanged corner). The fairway's right edge is the out-of-bounds line — a stone wall and white stakes, with no semi-rough at all on that side: the edge is absolute, which is the point. The left edge is rounded and wide, with 10 yards of semi-rough before the trees.
- **Bunkers:** The Road Hole bunker — a deep, revetted pot bunker on the front-left at 308 (moved out with the green) — from which the only play is out sideways. One fairway bunker right at 290, guarding the corner.
- **Green:** Narrow, 20 yards wide, guarded by the Road Hole bunker. A stone wall lines the back of the green; anything long is a penalty. The green slopes back-to-front at 2%, with a back-right shelf that is the Sunday pin.
- **Wind:** Across the wind. The tee shot decides the hole. **School:** Heroic with a penal edge. **Template:** Road Hole. **Inspiration:** the 17th at St. Andrews — the hardest hole in the world, at Wyatt National scale. **Identity preserved:** still the Road Hole, still the dare.

### 18. The Closer — Par 4, 430 yards, uphill to a clubhouse green (was 410)
- **The question:** Can you finish in front of everyone?
- **Slope/elevation:** 34 feet uphill (was 30) to a clubhouse green, with the gallery behind it. The approach is hit toward the people — the broadcast's closing shot.
- **Fairway shape:** 38 yards wide, edges rounded and generous. The fairway cambers gently toward the bunker at 300 — the aiming point — so a drive at the bunker kicks forward down the slope.
- **Bunkers:** Fairway bunker at 300 (driving distance, unchanged), deep and flashed; one greenside bunker right at 404.
- **Green:** Wide and welcoming, the largest on the back nine, sloping back-to-front at 2%, with a false front and a back tier. Front pins are birdie looks; back pins demand the approach land short and release.
- **Wind:** Helping. A birdie to finish, in front of everyone. **School:** Strategic.

## Routing notes (unchanged from v2, with v3 additions)

- **Two loops of nine** (MacKenzie principle 1): both nines start and end at the clubhouse.
- **Wind rotation:** holes run in every compass direction; no two consecutive holes play the same wind. The prevailing southwest wind helps on 1, 5, 9, 15, and 18; hurts on 4, 8, 11, and 13; crosses on 6, 10, 16, and 17.
- **Elevation rhythm:** the course climbs and falls in waves — downhill opener, uphill examination, valley par 5, downhill signature par 3, uphill grinder, uphill closer. The v3 climbs are longer and steeper; no hill climbing for its own sake (MacKenzie principle 4).
- **Difficulty arc:** the front nine opens gently and hardens (4, 8); the back nine opens with a test (10, 11), breathes at the signature par 3 (12), grinds (13), gambles (14, 15), tests (16, 17), and closes with a scoring chance (18). **Stroke index order (provisional, re-rank after calibration):** 13, 4, 11, 17, 8, 6, 2, 16, 10, 14, 7, 1, 15, 3, 5, 9, 18, 12 — same order as v2, flagged for re-derivation because 4 and 13 got harder and 11 got longer.
- **Water in play on six holes:** 2 (lake), 8 (pond), 12 (pond), 14 (creek), plus the bunker complexes on 15 and 17 that play like water. Water is functional, never decorative: it always guards the bold line, never the safe one.
- **Fairway-edge language (new in v3):** every fairway has a stated width at the landing area, a semi-rough band width (4–10 yards, wider on the forgiving side, narrower on the hazard side), and a camber direction. The mow line is scalloped, never straight — the fairway bulges toward the bold line and pinches toward the hazard.

## What makes it great (v3)

- **It is a championship course now.** 7,602 yards, par 72, with a 624-yard par 5, a 482-yard par 4, and a 238-yard par 3 into the wind. The length is real, and it is earned — every added yard lengthens an approach or a decision, never a walk.
- **The ground is the defense.** Slope, camber, rounded edges, and semi-rough do the work that rough and water used to do. The course reads as land, not as a corridor.
- **The greens are characters.** Every green has a stated slope, a tier structure, and a contour personality — and the putts on them are readable from the fairway and unreadable from the tee.
- **The identity holes are untouched in character.** The 5th is still drivable, the 12th is still a wedge over water, the 17th is still the Road Hole dare, the 18th is still the closer in front of everyone. Lengthening them made them more themselves, not less.
- **The Road to 59 chase gets harder — and therefore more credible.** On 7,214 yards a 59 needed a near-perfect day. On 7,602 yards it needs a near-perfect day on a harder course, which is exactly why the chase is worth watching.

## 5. What changes in the sim (spec only — no code changed here)

This section is the implementation brief for `sim/course.py`, `sim/constants.py`, and the calibration harness. It is a spec, not a code change.

### 5.1 `sim/course.py` — `build_wyatt_national()`

- **New yardages** for all 18 holes (per the tables above). Azimuths, hole names, and dogleg positions/angles are unchanged — the routing is the same.
- **Elevations:** update `elev_green_ft` where v3 changes the climb — 1st: −8 → −10 (tee higher); 4th: +28 → +34; 7th: +18 → +22; 9th: −12 → −14; 10th: −6 → −8; 11th: +30 → +34; 12th: −15 → −17; 13th: +22 → +28; 18th: +16 → +19. All other `elev_tee_ft`/`elev_green_ft` values unchanged. (`elevation_at()` interpolates linearly, so these changes steepen the climbs on 4, 11, and 13 as designed.)
- **Bunker positions:** driving-distance bunkers stay at their current `s_yd` (270–300) because Mas's driving distance does not change. Greenside bunkers move outward with the green — add the hole's yardage gain to their `s_yd` (e.g. 1st greenside bunker 385 → 405; 4th greenside 420 → 448; 9th front-right 375 → 397; 18th greenside 385 → 405). Cross-bunker on the 7th: 462 → **496** (stays 90 yards short of the green). Creek on the 14th: stays at 280. **New bunker:** 11th, fairway bunker left at 340 (`Bunker(340, -16, 26, 14)`).
- **Green radius:** greens grow slightly on the holes whose approaches get longest, so a well-struck long iron still has a target — 4th: 42 → 45 ft; 11th: 42 → 45 ft; 13th: 42 → 44 ft; 15th: 42 → 44 ft. All other `green_radius_ft` unchanged. (The sim's `green_radius_ft` is a circle; the doc's green sizes are the design intent — the radius change is the sim's approximation of "bigger greens on longer holes".)
- **Fairway widths:** mostly unchanged (`fairway_width_yd` default 32). The 13th narrows: set `fairway_width_yd=28.0` on hole 13 (the narrowest on the course).
- **NEW — semi-rough band:** add a `semi_rough_yd: float = 7.0` field to `Hole` (default 7, per-hole overrides: 5th left 10 / right 5; 6th left 10 / right 5 — the sim's single value is the average, so set 7.5 on 6; 10th left 10 / right 4 → 7.0; 13th 5.0; 17th left 10 / right 0 — the OB side has no semi-rough, so set 5.0 and note the OB side is absolute). `Course.determine_lie()` gains a `semi` lie: lateral distance within `fairway_width_yd/2 + semi_rough_yd` is semi-rough, with a lie effect between fairway and rough (specify in `sim/constants.py` lie table: distance_factor 0.97, spin_factor 0.95, dispersion multiplier 1.15 — values to be confirmed against the existing lie table, not invented as final). This is the sim's version of the rounded fairway edge: there is no longer a hard line where fairway becomes rough.
- **Rating/slope:** `compute_rating_slope()` recomputes from the new design — no code change, but the output changes. ~~Expect the rating to rise roughly 0.8–1.2 strokes and the slope to rise roughly 4–7 points from the v1 numbers.~~ **Superseded by the recompute — see `docs/course-rating-v3.md`: the actual rise is +1.5 rating (74.6 → 76.1) and +4 slope (138 → 142).** **Honest note:** two different v1 rating/slope pairs exist in the record — the web build shows 74.1/141 and `docs/conditions-system.md` derives 74.6/138. The v3 recompute must be run once and the single resulting number used everywhere; do not carry both forward.

### 5.2 `sim/course.py` — `championship_variant()`

- **The Black Tees variant is retired as specified.** v3 *is* the championship length — a +350-yard variant on top of a 7,602-yard course would be 7,952 yards, which is not a golf course, it is a hike. Two options, implementer's choice: (a) delete `championship_variant()` and the `wyatt_national_black` course id; or (b) re-baseline it as a **tournament setup** on the v3 base — same yardages, fairways narrowed to 26 yards, greens to 36 ft, and one extra fairway bunker at 300 yards on every par 4 and par 5 (the existing narrowing logic, without the +350 yards). Option (b) is preferred: it preserves the "Major week" setup tier in `docs/conditions-system.md` without inventing a longer course.

### 5.3 Scoring expectations — what the longer course does to a +3

Driving does not change: Mas's club speed, ball speed, and driving distance band (**265–275 yards**) are properties of the golfer, not the course. Everything downstream gets harder:

- **Approaches get longer.** Mean approach distance on par 4s grows roughly 15–20 yards (the par-4 gain is +214 yards across ten holes, and the drive does not cover it). On par 5s the second shot grows ~30 yards and the third shot stays at the lay-up number, so par-5 scoring is less affected than par-4 scoring.
- **Proximity worsens.** Per the sim's approach truth tables (`sim/constants.py` §3.3), a 150-yard approach averages ~24–28 feet; a 175-yard approach averages ~30–35 feet. The extra 15–20 yards on par-4 approaches moves a meaningful share of approaches from the 125–150 bucket into the 150–175 and 175–200 buckets.
- **GIR falls.** The v1 calibration target is ~60% GIR. On v3, expect **~55–57%** — a 3–5 point drop, concentrated on the long par 4s (4th, 13th) and the long par 5s' third shots.
- **Scoring band moves up.** The v1 band is **71–73** (mean ~72). On v3 the plausible band is **72–74, mean ~73.0–73.5** — roughly one stroke harder. The band is an estimate, not a measurement: it must be confirmed by the calibration harness (below), and if the harness says 73–75, the harness is right and this paragraph is wrong.
- **The 59 gets rarer.** On v1, P(59) on a daily setup is ~0.5%. On v3, expect **~0.3%** — the chase stays credible (a 59 is still possible on a near-perfect day) but the "closest to 59" board gets more entries in the 60–62 range and fewer at 59. The 59 Watch pace line is unchanged (par 72), but the live probability model in `sim/fifty_nine_watch.py` must be re-derived from v3 rounds — the Monte Carlo over remaining holes runs through the new course data, so this happens automatically once the course is rebuilt; the *calibration* of that probability (how often the model says 20% and it actually happens 20% of the time) needs re-running.
- **Strokes-gained profile shifts.** Long game and approach play gain weight relative to putting: the extra length is all in the full-swing game. The learning loop's "one fix" will tilt toward approach and driving for the first few seasons on v3, which is correct — that is where the strokes went.

### 5.4 What must be re-run in `docs/qa-plan.md`

- **§1 calibration harness (10,000 rounds) — mandatory re-run.** Every target in the harness table is course-dependent except driving distance. The harness must be re-run against the v3 course before v3 ships, and the following targets re-derived from the results:
  - Scoring average: 71–73 → expect 72–74 (confirm from the harness, do not assert).
  - GIR: ~60% → expect ~55–57%.
  - Fairways: 55–60% — unchanged in expectation (driving is unchanged and fairway widths are mostly unchanged), but the 13th's narrower fairway and the semi-rough band change the *definition* of a fairway hit, so the band must be re-measured, not assumed.
  - Driving distance: 265–275 — **unchanged, no re-derivation needed**; it is the one target the course does not touch.
  - Putts per round and scrambling: re-derive — longer approaches mean longer first putts (putts may rise ~0.5/round) and more missed greens (scrambling opportunities rise, scrambling rate may fall).
- **R-03 (the low-round gate) — re-check.** "Any round ≤ 61 in the harness has GIR ≥ 15, putts ≤ 26, zero penalty strokes, and no putt holed from > 25 ft." On a longer course a 61 is a better round than on v1 — the gate's *conditions* still hold, but the *frequency* of rounds ≤ 61 will fall. The companion gate — "≥1 round ≤ 62 in 10,000 but 0–2 rounds ≤ 59" — must be re-checked: expect the ≤ 62 count to fall and the ≤ 59 count to stay at 0–1.
- **Decision-engine EV tables — re-derive.** The lay-up numbers on 2, 7, 11, and 15 change (the 7th cross-bunker moved to 496; the 11th has a new bunker at 340; the 2nd and 15th second shots are 33–34 yards longer). The Monte Carlo decision engine re-derives these automatically from the new course data, but the *documented* lay-up numbers in `docs/sim-engine.md` §3 (100 yards on the 7th, the 11th's three-shot routing) must be re-verified against the new runs.
- **Conditions-system setup ratings — re-derive.** `docs/conditions-system.md` §4 setup tiers (Daily 74.6/138, Championship 75.4/141, Major week 76.2/145, Soft 73.8/134) are built on the v1 rating. ~~All four tiers shift up by the v3 rating delta (roughly +0.8–1.2). The tier *spreads* stay the same; the absolute numbers move.**
- **Fifty-nine Watch probabilities — re-derive.** The 500-run Monte Carlo over remaining holes uses the new course automatically, but the documented P(59) figures (~0.5% daily, ~0.02% major week) must be re-measured from v3 harness rounds.

The web build's course data (hole yardages, par, names) must be updated to v3 — that is a data change in the artifact, not a re-verification of the sim.
